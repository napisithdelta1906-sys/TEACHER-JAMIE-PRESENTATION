Part One · The Man

Patrick was not Irish.

He was born in Roman Britain around the year 385. His own writings call him Patricius. He did not start life as a saint — he started it as an ordinary boy.

c. 385

Born in Roman Britain, likely in a family with some standing.

Faith

He later became one of the most important missionaries in history.

The Hard Years

Patrick on the Hill of Tara — the ancient heart of Ireland. Photo: Wikimedia Commons.

Raiders captured him and brought him to Ireland. For six years he herded sheep alone on a mountainside. It was a lonely, difficult time — but it shaped who he would become.

05
The Escape

He ran for his freedom.

After six years, Patrick escaped. He travelled about two hundred miles on foot to reach the coast, then found a ship that carried him home. He was free at last.

06
The Return

Then he chose to go back.

Back home, Patrick had a dream. He heard the voices of the Irish people calling him to return. So he went back — not as a slave, but as a missionary, to share his faith with the very people among whom he had suffered.

The Dream

A vision of the Irish people asking him to come back and walk among them.

c. 432

He returned to Ireland and spent the rest of his life there.

His journey ended where it began — tradition says he rests at Downpatrick. Photo: Wikimedia Commons.

Part Two · The Symbols

The little plant with three leaves.

Patrick is said to have used the shamrock to explain the idea of the Trinity — three parts, one whole. It became the most famous symbol of Ireland.

One stem, three leaves. Hover over each leaf to see it light up gold.

08
The Color Story

It used to be blue.

Early pictures show Patrick in blue. Green took over in the 1790s, when Irish people adopted it as the color of their identity. Ireland's green hills made the choice feel natural.

10
Truth Behind the Myth

Look closely at this snake.

Now here is the surprise: there are no wild snakes in Ireland. Not one. There never have been. After the last Ice Age, the sea kept every snake out.

The Legend

Patrick banished every snake from the island into the sea.

The Fact

Ireland never had snakes — so there was nothing to banish.

The Meaning

The story likely symbolizes replacing old beliefs with the new faith.

0 snakes in Ireland

A grass snake (Natrix natrix) — Wikimedia Commons Picture of the Day. Beautiful, right? You will never meet one in the wild in Ireland.

The Holy Mountain

Forty days on the summit.

Patrick is said to have fasted for forty days on the top of Croagh Patrick. Today, thousands of pilgrims still climb it every year, and many make the journey barefoot as an act of devotion.

The First Parade

It started in Florida.

The earliest recorded St. Patrick's Day parade was not in Ireland or New York. It took place in 1601 in the Spanish colony of St. Augustine, Florida, led by an Irish vicar.

Part Four · The Living Tradition

The world turns green.

Since 2010, Tourism Ireland has invited landmarks around the world to light up green on the evening of March 17. Hundreds of sites now take part.

Food and Drink

Eat like the Irish.

Corned beef and cabbage is actually an Irish-American invention. In Ireland, the traditional dishes are soda bread, colcannon, boxty, and slow-cooked stew.
